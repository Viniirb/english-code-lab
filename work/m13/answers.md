# My answers

## Q1
Situation: Last year our checkout page showed a wrong total for some users, and many of them left the page without buying.
Task: My job was to find the cause quickly and fix it before the weekend sale started.
Action: I read the logs, I wrote a test that showed the problem, and I found that the discount was applied two times. I fixed the code and I asked a teammate to review it.
Result: The fix went to production in one day, and the number of failed payments went down by 30 percent.

## Q2
Situation: In my last project, four developers and one designer built a mobile app in six weeks, and we needed to share the work well.
Task: I was responsible for the login and the profile screens, and I had to keep the code easy for others to use.
Action: I wrote a short plan, I made small pull requests every day, and I helped two teammates when they had problems with my screens.
Result: We finished the app one week early, and the team used my login code in 3 other projects.

## Q3
Situation: My company chose a new framework, and nobody in my team knew it, but we had only two weeks before the first demo.
Task: I wanted to learn it fast, so I could build the first screens and teach the others.
Action: I read the official guide, I built a small test app in two days, and I wrote notes. Then I shared the notes and I did a short class for my team.
Result: We showed a working demo after ten days, and the client approved the project and asked for 5 new features.

## Q4
Situation: Once I deleted a database table by mistake while I was cleaning a test server, and two developers lost their data.
Task: I had to restore the data fast and make sure that this problem would not happen again.
Action: I told the team at once, I restored a backup, and I wrote a checklist for safe changes. I also asked for a separate account with fewer rights for the test server, so nobody could delete important data again.
Result: Everything was back in 40 minutes, and we had zero similar problems in the next six months.
